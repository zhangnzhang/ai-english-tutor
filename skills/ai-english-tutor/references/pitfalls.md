# Pitfalls from real sessions

Each of these happened during daily tutoring of one Chinese-speaking adult preparing for IELTS. They are kept because the fix is not obvious until you have made the mistake.

## Teaching

**An abstract model did not land; an analogy from a word they already knew did.**
Explaining *have done* versus *had done* with "three points on a timeline" pushed the learner toward *had* everywhere. Switching to "*have* = you own a result now, *had* = you owned it then" (from *have a car / had a car*) fixed the next set.
Fix: reach for an analogy built on something the learner already uses correctly.

**Four points taught at once, first set 3/8.**
*suggest that…*, *suggest doing*, *recommend* and *it's important that…* in one explanation. The learner's native pattern ("suggest you to do") took over.
Fix: one safe pattern first. Drill until stable. Add the second later, and give an everyday substitute they already control (*I think you should…*) in the meantime.

**The second pattern was added too early.**
After *suggest doing* was 4/4, adding *suggest we do* in the very next set made *-ing* spread to the new slot.
Fix: let a pattern survive at least one spaced review before adding its neighbour.

**Testing a "known" unit without re-explaining it.**
A week after a unit, a diagnostic was given after three lines of recap. The learner asked four "why" questions in a row and then said nobody had explained it.
Fix: having studied something is not the same as remembering why. Explain the core and give two examples per rule before every diagnostic.

**A new structure contaminated an old one.**
After the present perfect continuous (*have been doing*), the learner added *been* to every perfect form and scored 4/10 on a mixed test.
Fix: run a "new or old?" set before any mixed test.

**A one-shot list of irregular verbs: 2/8. The same verbs grouped by pattern: 9/10.**
Fix: group by pattern (sing–sang–sung with ring, drink…), never alphabetically.

## Question writing

**Two correct options.**
*It's time ___ your medicine.* with options *for* and *to take*. Both are fine English.
Fix: substitute every option and read the sentence before sending.

**The stated word count pointed at a wrong answer.**
A blank marked "2 words" where the real answer was one word made the learner write *have learned* next to *yesterday*.
Fix: count the words of the actual answer before stating a count.

**No time signal in a tense question.**
*"___ the hotel nice?"* accepts both *Is* and *Was*. *today / this morning / this year* are ambiguous for perfect-versus-past.
Fix: every tense question carries a clear time word or event.

**The native-language hint carried the error.**
For Chinese speakers, a hint containing only 已经 ("already") maps straight to *had*.
Fix: write the hint so it states the time frame.

**Answers clustered in the first positions.**
The learner noticed the correct option was almost always 1–3.
Fix: check the distribution of answer positions for each set.

**A unit quiz that tested earlier units.**
A quiz on *have* mixed past, perfect and continuous forms. The learner failed it twice for reasons unrelated to the unit.
Fix: unit quizzes test the unit. Mixing belongs in reviews.

**An unnatural question produced an unusable answer.**
A short-answer question phrased awkwardly got an answer to a different question.
Fix: read each question as plain English before sending.

## Grading

**Reasons were merged into one paragraph when there were many errors.**
The learner noticed immediately and called it lazy.
Fix: one line of why under every wrong answer, always.

**Dictated answers were graded as grammar errors.**
*walk* arrived as *working*, a city name arrived as a different word.
Fix: a similar-sounding real word is a recognition error; a non-word is a spelling error; judge level from typed text only.

**A blank filled with only the auxiliary.**
The learner wrote *doesn't* and left out the verb.
Fix: ask for the complete content of the blank.

## Pace

**Accuracy fell from 6/8 to 3/8 after more than ten sets in one day.**
Fix: narrow to one point and keep going rather than adding material; open the next session with that day's mistakes.

**Required vocabulary in free writing was ignored four times in a row.**
Fix: stop requiring it there. Recycle the words in drills and dictation instead.
